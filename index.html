About the work

Takagi Myakano is the author behind Japanese Philosophy for Inner Peace, a series of short, practical books about living with more attention and less noise.

Each book takes a single Japanese concept — ikigai, wabi-sabi, kintsugi, kaizen — and carries it from philosophy into practice: what it means, why it has lasted, and what to do with it on an ordinary Tuesday. The aim is plain guidance you can act on the same day you read it.

The thread running through all four is the same. A calmer, more meaningful life is not built by a single decision but by small, repeatable ones: finding a reason to get up, accepting what is imperfect, mending what has broken, and improving by degrees. The series exists to make those ideas usable, and to keep them short enough to finish.

The author

Takagi Myakano did not set out to become an author. He set out to understand a single question: what makes a life feel whole?

For years, he immersed himself in the quiet corners of Japanese thought — sitting with the idea of ikigai, a reason for being; studying wabi-sabi, the beauty found in imperfection; tracing the gold seams of kintsugi, where breaking becomes a kind of repair; and practicing kaizen, the discipline of small, steady change. He read what the philosophers wrote, then set the books aside and tried to live what they meant.

Somewhere along the way, students began coming to him instead. Some call him a teacher. A few, only half-joking, call him a master. Takagi shrugs off both. He still thinks of himself as a student who never quite finishes learning.

His books are an attempt to pass along what took him years to learn, in plain language, for anyone ready to begin.
